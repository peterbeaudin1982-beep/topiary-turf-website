Our story

Rooted in the Bluegrass

Peter didn't come to Kentucky by accident. He came for the land. The first time he drove the back roads outside Lexington in late spring, past the stone walls and the horse farms and the bluegrass rolling under old oaks, it felt like the countryside was being cared for by people who meant to hand it down. He decided he wanted to do that kind of work.

He had spent years around landscaping crews that worked fast and sprayed everything. Lawns came out looking fine and soil came out tired. When he moved to the Bluegrass and started Topiary & Turf Estate Care, he set two rules on day one: do the work by hand, and do it without chemicals.

"The ground here has been good to people for a very long time. The least we can do is be good to it back."

The soil beneath the Bluegrass

Central Kentucky sits on some of the most famous ground in the country. Beneath our lawns lies ancient limestone. Over thousands of years it has given the soil a natural richness in calcium and phosphorus, and our pastures, our horses and even our springs owe their character to it.

That's a gift, and a responsibility. Limestone country is porous. What goes onto a lawn in Lexington doesn't always stay there. It can travel through the ground into the creeks and springs that run under the city. Peter believes the most respectful thing a landscaper can do with soil this good is leave it be: feed it with mulch and compost, keep it covered, cut grass at the right height, and let biology do the rest.

Craftsmanship over shortcuts

The "topiary" in our name is a promise of patience. A well-shaped boxwood takes seasons, not an afternoon. A healthy dogwood needs the right cut in the right month. A lawn that holds up through a Kentucky July is built underground, slowly. We'd rather do fewer properties beautifully than many in a hurry.

So our crews carry sharp shears and well-tuned mowers, not spray tanks. We pull weeds. We edge beds by hand. We take clippings home to compost. And we leave every property quieter, cleaner and a little healthier than we found it.

— Peter, Founder
